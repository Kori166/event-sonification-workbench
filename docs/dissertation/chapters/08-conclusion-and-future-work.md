# 8. Conclusion and Future Work

This project developed a deterministic workbench that transforms public tracking annotations into a common event representation, maps eligible observations to traceable audio cues, renders sample-verifiable audio and integrates the resulting evidence for inspection. Across MOT17-02-DPM and KITTI Tracking 0000, the pipeline accounted for every valid event, achieved 100% eligible-event coverage and full cue and suppression traceability, and placed all cue boundaries at the expected integer samples. Repeated builds were byte identical within the recorded Windows/AMD64/Python 3.14.3 environment.

The three research questions were answered at a technical level. Heterogeneous datasets can be normalised through a stable common schema that preserves source-specific semantics and provenance. Visual events can be mapped deterministically through explicit spatial and temporal functions, content-derived identities and recorded suppression decisions. Outputs can be evaluated through predeclared measures that distinguish accounting from coverage, samples from decimal seconds, verified traces from plausible identifiers, and environment-bounded reproducibility from broader portability.

Against the original objectives, O1 and O2 were achieved. O3 and O4 were partially achieved: the deterministic mapping, renderer, export and technical evaluation are complete, but the proposed additional presets, density controls, ablations and some originally named measures were not implemented.

The evaluation also identifies the principal limitation of the baseline. Technical completeness did not prevent severe output density: MOT17 produced 1,342.18 cues per second and a normalised overlap burden of 160.06. No participant or perceptual study was conducted, so the work does not establish that such output is distinguishable, usable or accessible.

Future work should first evaluate density-control policies such as track-level aggregation, priority, refractory intervals and polyphony limits, using controlled comparisons against the frozen baseline. Alternative mappings should examine height, temporal smoothing and KITTI's three-dimensional information rather than relying only on bounding-box area as an apparent-scale proxy. A participant study could then test discrimination, task performance, workload and accessibility with appropriate ethics approval. Finally, the reproducibility study should be extended across operating systems and runtime versions, and the common schema should be challenged with additional annotation modalities and datasets.
